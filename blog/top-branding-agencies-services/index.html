Planning an E-commerce Website for a UAE Brand

NP NexPixels editorial · Updated 12 October 2026
E-commerce

An e-commerce brand experience depends on how products are explained and found, as well as their visual presentation. UAE customers need clear categories, useful product information and a recognisable tone. Design and catalogue decisions should support the same shopping journey.

Build the brief around your catalogue

Review how the brand appears across navigation, collection pages, product details and cart feedback. Consistent content and imagery help customers understand the range. Use reusable interface patterns while retaining the information that makes individual products distinct.

Practical review checklist

  • Document product information and image requirements.
  • Identify supported payment and shipping providers.
  • Explain inventory ownership and required ERP or POS connections.
  • Agree launch testing and post-launch maintenance responsibilities.

Organise the catalogue before designing pages

Navigation and filtering depend on how products are structured. List your categories, the attributes customers use to choose between products (size, colour, material, compatibility) and how many variants each product has. Clean, consistent product data makes the store easier to browse, search and maintain, and it simplifies future integrations with marketplaces or inventory systems.

Design for mobile shoppers first

In the UAE, a large share of browsing and buying happens on phones. Design the mobile category page, product page, cart and checkout before the desktop layouts. Keep tap targets large, make filters easy to open and close, and avoid long forms. Test on real devices over mobile data, not only on office Wi-Fi.

Plan payments, delivery and returns

Decide early which payment methods you will offer — cards, digital wallets, instalment providers or cash on delivery — and confirm they are supported by your platform. Define delivery zones and timeframes for Dubai, other emirates and GCC countries, along with how fees are calculated. Publish a clear returns policy and link it from product pages and checkout.

  • Will prices be shown inclusive of VAT, and how will invoices be issued?
  • Do you need English and Arabic storefronts at launch?
  • Which systems need to connect: accounting, inventory, courier or CRM?
  • Who will process orders and handle customer messages daily?

Content that supports the brand

Strong product photography, consistent descriptions and a clear brand story help a new store build trust. Prepare content in parallel with design so launch is not delayed by missing images or copy. Create templates for product descriptions so new items follow the same structure.

Measure after launch

Set up analytics and e-commerce tracking before going live so you can see where shoppers drop off. Review search terms, abandoned carts and the most-viewed products in the first weeks, and use these findings to prioritise improvements rather than redesigning based on opinion.

Set clear delivery priorities

Prepare a content and asset brief before redesigning the storefront. Identify catalogue owners, required photography and the rules for product naming. Connect approved visual decisions to the platform’s editing workflow so the brand remains consistent after launch. Explore development services or review selected projects.
