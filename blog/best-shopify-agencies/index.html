How to Choose a Shopify Developer in Dubai

NP NexPixels editorial · Updated 12 October 2026
Shopify

A Shopify developer in Dubai should be able to explain both the customer journey and the store’s content structure. Review collection navigation, product options and theme editing as well as visual design. Ask which features use custom Liquid and which rely on paid applications.

Review the whole shopping journey

Walk through discovery, product selection, cart and checkout on a mobile device. Discuss payment and delivery requirements with the proposed provider configuration. When migrating a store, review product data, important URLs and redirects before work begins.

Practical review checklist

  • Ask how custom Liquid sections and metafields will be maintained.
  • Review migration scope, including product data and URL redirects.
  • Confirm which payment and shipping integrations are included.
  • Agree testing, handover and post-launch support responsibilities.

Theme customisation versus custom development

Most Shopify projects start from a theme. The question is how far it will be adapted. Small changes — colours, typography, section order — can be handled in the theme editor. Larger requirements, such as bundled products, custom filters, size guides tied to product data or bilingual content, usually need custom Liquid sections and metafields. Ask the developer to label each requirement as theme setting, custom code or third-party app so you can see where ongoing costs and maintenance will come from.

Be deliberate about apps

Apps are convenient but each one adds monthly fees, scripts that affect page speed and another vendor with access to store data. A good developer will suggest removing apps where a small amount of theme code achieves the same result, and will explain the risks of apps that inject code into the theme. Keep a list of installed apps, what each does and who requested it.

Payments, delivery and local expectations

For stores selling in the UAE, confirm which payment gateways are available for your business type, whether cash on delivery is required, and how VAT will be shown on product pages and invoices. Delivery options often include same-day zones in Dubai and longer timelines to other emirates or GCC countries. These rules should be configured and tested before launch, not added after the first customer complaint.

Migrating an existing store

If you are moving from WooCommerce, Magento or another platform, the migration plan matters as much as the new design. It should cover product data and images, customer accounts, order history where needed, and a full list of old URLs with 301 redirects to their new equivalents. Without redirects, search rankings and bookmarked links can be lost overnight.

  • Export and review product data before it is imported, including variants and SKUs.
  • Map every important old URL to its new Shopify path.
  • Place test orders using each payment and shipping method.
  • Confirm email notifications, tax settings and order fulfilment steps.

After launch

Agree what support looks like for the first weeks after going live — bug fixes, small content changes and help with seasonal campaigns. Ask for short documentation on editing sections, managing metafields and safely updating the theme, so your team is not dependent on the developer for routine work.

Prepare your store requirements

Prepare your catalogue, current platform, required integrations and staff editing needs. Identify the launch essentials and the features that can follow later. A written brief helps the developer separate theme work, migration, third-party costs and ongoing support. Explore development services or review selected projects.
