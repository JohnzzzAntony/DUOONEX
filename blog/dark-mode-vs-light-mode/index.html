Dark or Light Mode: Choosing Readable Website UI

NP NexPixels editorial · Updated 12 October 2026
UI UX

Choose dark or light interface styling according to content, readability and the environment in which people use the product. Neither mode is automatically better for a UAE business website. Product imagery, forms and dense operational screens should be tested in the intended setting.

Prioritise readability and content

Review contrast, small text, input boundaries and selected states using realistic content. A theme that works for a large heading may fail in a table or validation message. Keep keyboard focus and error feedback visible across the full interaction, not just the initial screen.

Practical review checklist

  • Review text contrast across default, hover and disabled states.
  • Check product images against each background treatment.
  • Keep validation messages and keyboard focus easy to distinguish.
  • Test on actual mobile screens in different lighting conditions.

When dark interfaces work well

Dark themes suit products used for long sessions in low light, media-heavy experiences such as video or photography portfolios, and dashboards where charts and status colours need to stand out. They can also support a brand that is deliberately premium or technical. The benefit comes from careful contrast, not simply inverting colours: pure black backgrounds with pure white text can cause glare and make thin fonts harder to read.

When light interfaces are the safer default

Content-heavy pages, long articles, forms and e-commerce catalogues are usually easier to read on light backgrounds, particularly in bright environments. Product photography is often shot on white or neutral backgrounds and can look disconnected on dark surfaces. For most business websites, a light theme with strong typography is the lower-risk starting point.

Offering both modes

If you support both themes, respect the visitor’s system preference using the prefers-color-scheme media query and give them a visible toggle. Store their choice so it persists between visits. Every component — tables, charts, form errors, code samples, embedded maps, logos — needs to be designed and tested in each mode. Logos and illustrations often need alternate versions.

Contrast and accessibility rules

Aim for at least the WCAG 2.2 AA contrast ratios: 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text and essential interface elements such as input borders and focus outlines. Dark themes commonly fail on secondary text and disabled states, while light themes often fail on pale grey placeholders. Check contrast with real content, not just colour swatches.

  • Use off-black and off-white rather than extreme values to reduce glare.
  • Increase font weight slightly for light text on dark backgrounds.
  • Avoid relying on shadows for depth in dark mode; use surface tones instead.
  • Re-check charts and status colours so meaning is preserved in each theme.

Make the decision with users

If you are unsure, prototype both directions and test them with a handful of real users in the conditions where they will use the product — an office, a warehouse floor or a phone outdoors in daylight. The result should be guided by readability and task completion rather than personal preference.

Document the design rules

Document the rules for text, surfaces, borders and interaction states in the design system. If both modes are offered, review every important component in each mode.
