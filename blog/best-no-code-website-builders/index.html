Choosing a Website Platform for Your UAE Business

NP NexPixels editorial · Updated 12 October 2026
Web design

Choose a website platform around the work your UAE business needs to do. A publishing site, an online store and a staff application need different editing, data and integration capabilities. Start with those requirements before comparing templates or subscription prices.

Match the platform to the workflow

Shopify centres the workflow on commerce; WordPress supports structured publishing; custom development provides control over application behaviour. Check the specific features and integrations your business requires on the proposed setup. Platform choice should include the people responsible for maintaining it.

Practical review checklist

  • List who will edit products, pages and operational records.
  • Check required payment, shipping and business-system integrations.
  • Compare recurring platform, app, hosting and maintenance costs.
  • Plan ownership, data exports and migration before committing.

Separate the website from the business system

Many platform decisions go wrong because the website is expected to behave like an ERP, booking engine or CRM. Start by listing what the site must do on its own — publish pages, collect enquiries, sell products — and what should stay in other tools. A hosted builder can be ideal for a brochure site with a contact form, while a store with variants, stock locations and delivery rules usually needs an e-commerce platform. Custom development becomes sensible when the workflow is specific to your business and off-the-shelf tools would need heavy workarounds.

Compare the main platform types

  • Hosted website builders — quick to launch and easy to edit, but templates, SEO controls and integrations are limited to what the vendor provides.
  • WordPress — flexible publishing and a large plugin ecosystem; it needs disciplined updates, security hardening and reliable hosting.
  • Shopify — strong for product catalogues, checkout and payments; custom requirements are handled through Liquid themes, metafields and apps.
  • Custom builds (for example React or Next.js) — full control over content models and integrations, with higher initial cost and a need for a development partner.

Consider language, payments and local requirements

UAE businesses often need English and Arabic content, right-to-left layouts, VAT-inclusive pricing and local payment gateways. Check how each platform handles these before choosing it. Translation plugins and RTL themes vary in quality, and some payment providers only support certain platforms. If you plan to sell across the GCC, also review currency display, shipping zones and how duties or delivery fees are presented at checkout.

Think about who will edit the site

The best platform is one your team will actually maintain. Ask who will add pages, update prices, publish articles and respond to form submissions. A marketing team may prefer a visual editor, while an operations team may need bulk imports. Request a short demonstration of the editing experience with your own content types, and make sure user roles prevent accidental changes to important pages.

Plan for growth and exit

Check how easy it is to export content, products and customer data. Hosted builders can make migrations difficult, and heavily customised themes can lock you into one developer. Keep ownership of your domain, analytics and hosting accounts in the company’s name, and document which plugins or apps the site depends on.

Write a practical platform brief

Write down pages, products, account roles and essential integrations, then identify who owns each content area. Compare recurring platform, app, hosting and support costs.
