/ Project overview

Finora is a business accounting application designed around the daily work of UAE trading and service companies. Sales, purchases, expenses and inventory connect to a double-entry ledger. Teams can review invoices, payments, bank transactions and reports within the same organisation workspace.

The implementation uses Next.js, TypeScript and Prisma, with money stored in integer minor units. The source includes balanced journal posting, reversing entries, document numbering, inventory movements, UAE VAT handling and role-based access. The device mockups were captured from a local build running demonstration data for a fictional trading company; no customer or financial records are shown.

/ The customer journey

How the workflow works

Users move from a customer or supplier record to a commercial document, then to posting and settlement. Every invoice shows the journal entries it created, so staff can trace a balance back to its source. The dashboard summarises cash, receivables and payables and links overdue items to the underlying records.

  • Sales invoices, quotes and customer payments
  • Purchase orders, bills and vendor payments
  • Bank accounts, transfers and reconciliation
  • Inventory items, warehouses and valuation

/ Design and development focus

Design and development focus

Financial documents feed balanced journal entries rather than isolated dashboard totals. The code represents amounts in fils and handles corrections through reversing entries. Tenant context, roles and server-side validation are part of the implementation, alongside reporting, approvals and recurring documents.

  • Purchase orders, bills and vendor payments
  • Bank accounts, transfers and reconciliation
  • Inventory items, warehouses and valuation
  • P&L, balance sheet, trial balance and ageing reports
  • UAE VAT return view, roles and AI assistance

Implementation and scope

Developed application; not deployed to a public live URL. The documented scope includes accounting, inventory, banking, reports and account administration. The assistant and external services depend on their configured providers. Tax settings and financial reports need business-specific review before operational use; the portfolio does not represent regulatory certification.
